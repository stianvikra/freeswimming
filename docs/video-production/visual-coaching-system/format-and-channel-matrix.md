# Format And Channel Matrix

## Baseline Formats

All visual coaching assets and production rules must support:

- `16:9` course and website video,
- `9:16` vertical social video,
- `1:1` square social video.

The source edit should preserve enough crop room that the same lesson can produce all three outputs without redesigning the overlay language.

## Format Matrix

| Format | Primary output                           | Typical resolution         | Best use                                                                | Overlay density | Safe-zone rule                                                             |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `16:9` | Course, website, YouTube-style landscape | `1920x1080` or `3840x2160` | Full technique lessons, split-screen, before/after, detailed breakdowns | medium          | keep instructional overlays outside the central swimmer lane when possible |
| `9:16` | Shorts, Reels, TikTok-style clips        | `1080x1920`                | One cue, one mistake/correction, one drill teaser                       | low             | keep text inside the middle `80%` width and away from platform UI zones    |
| `1:1`  | Feed posts, ads, compact previews        | `1080x1080`                | teaser, before/after, short comparison, practice cue                    | low to medium   | keep swimmer centered and avoid crowding top/bottom with stacked labels    |

## Production Strategy

1. Shoot and edit with a `16:9` course master in mind when possible.
2. Keep swimmer framing crop-safe for `9:16` and `1:1`.
3. Design overlays from a shared component family.
4. Adapt placement and density per format.
5. Export each format with explicit filename suffixes.

## Crop-Safe Framing

### Underwater Side

- Keep the full body line visible for `16:9`.
- Preserve enough center framing that `9:16` can show head, torso, hips, and lead arm at the teaching moment.
- Avoid placing the swimmer too close to the left or right edge when a social crop may be needed.

### Underwater Front

- Keep hands, shoulders, head, and lane reference centered.
- Use this angle for symmetry, alignment, and breathing cues.
- Avoid wide horizontal callouts that will not survive `9:16`.

### Above-Water iPhone/iPad

- Use for rhythm, breathing timing, recovery, entry, and whole-stroke context.
- Keep horizon and wall lines level where practical.
- Capture extra top/bottom room if a vertical social cutdown is likely.

### Low Visibility Or Poor Light

- Prefer fewer overlays.
- Use higher contrast text and thicker lines.
- Avoid small angle labels or subtle transparent guides.
- Do not force a clip into social format if the key motion becomes unreadable.

### Wall And Lane-Line Shots

- Use wall shots for push-off, turn timing, and optional wall-logo treatment.
- Use lane lines as alignment references only when they help the cue.
- Do not place brand marks on walls unless the effect is deliberate and verified.

## Overlay Placement By Format

### `16:9`

- Title and objective: upper left or centered on a clean intro frame.
- Coaching callouts: left/right thirds, away from the swimmer's face and hands.
- Captions: lower safe zone, unless a coaching lower third is active.
- Split-screen labels: top of each panel.
- Watermark: top right.

### `9:16`

- One primary coaching cue at a time.
- Text should usually sit above or below the active motion, not across the body.
- Captions and coaching labels must be separated vertically.
- Avoid more than two simultaneous overlay elements.
- Watermark may move to the least busy upper safe corner.

### `1:1`

- Keep cue labels short.
- Use centered or top-left title placement only when swimmer visibility remains clear.
- Split-screen should be simple: top/bottom or left/right depending on motion.
- Watermark should stay small and out of the body line.

## Format-Specific Anti-Patterns

- `16:9`: filling the wide frame with too many labels because there is room.
- `9:16`: shrinking a landscape lesson into vertical without rethinking text and crop.
- `1:1`: using both captions and coaching callouts in the same bottom area.
- All formats: placing overlays over the swimmer's head, hands, catch, body line, or wall contact.
